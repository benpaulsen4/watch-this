Series Finale is built entirely from what you've recorded in WatchThis. When a number looks wrong, it's usually down to how or when something was recorded.

## The Times on My Biggest Day Are Wrong

![The Biggest day panel, with a timeline and a list of episodes and times](/help/series-finale/recap-biggest-day.webp)

- **Times use your profile's timezone.** Everything in your Series Finale, including which day an episode lands on, uses the timezone in your profile, wherever you're viewing from. If your profile's timezone is wrong, so are the times. Read: [Edit Your Profile: Name, Photo, and Timezone](/help/profile/edit-your-profile).
- **The time is when you ticked it.** Series Finale knows when you *marked* an episode watched, not when you watched it. Ticking off last night's episode the next morning makes it a morning watch.

## My Biggest Day Has No Times

Times only come from episodes you marked watched **one at a time**. Marking a whole season (or several episodes) watched in one go stamps them all with the same moment, which says nothing about when you watched each one.

If fewer than 50 of the year's episodes were ticked one at a time, the biggest-day card shows the date and number of episodes without a timeline.

## My Hours Seem Low

Hours add up the runtime of every episode you watched and every film you finished, using runtimes from TMDB. If TMDB doesn't know how long something is, it's left out of your hours, and your recap says so under the headline: "Excludes N episodes or films with no runtime on TMDB".

## A Title Is in the Wrong Year

- **Finished titles count in the year you set them to Completed,** and dropped shows in the year you set them to **Dropped**. If you changed a title's status again later, it counts in the year of that later change.
- **Episodes count in the year you marked them watched.**

## My "Most Obscure Film" Isn't That Obscure

Obscurity is based on TMDB's popularity score as it was when your Series Finale was put together, not when you watched the film. A film can become better known (or less so) over time.

## The Genre Percentages Don't Add Up

Most titles have two or three genres on TMDB, so each percentage is that genre's share of all the genre tags across what you finished, not a share of the titles themselves. Because each figure is rounded separately, the total can also come to a little over or under 100%.

## I Fixed My History, but the Recap Didn't Change

Each Series Finale is a snapshot taken when it was first put together, so it stays the same afterwards, matching any card you've already shared. Changes you make to an earlier year's history afterwards won't appear in that year's recap.

## Only First Watches Count

WatchThis doesn't track rewatches, so each episode and title counts once.
